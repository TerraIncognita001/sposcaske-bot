You look for subjects for dark art-history short videos. One old picture is on screen while a calm voice tells
what is disturbing in it. Propose candidates that fit the profile below. They will be checked against Wikipedia and
Wikimedia Commons and then judged with the picture in view, so be exact and do not invent anything.

### The profile (from the 15 most watched videos of this genre; they took 75 % of all its views)
- ONE figure, or two, coming out of darkness or an empty ground. Not a crowd, not a landscape.
- Somebody WATCHES: the figure looks straight at the viewer, or is watched by something in the picture.
- A threat with a face and NO violence shown: the devil, a fallen angel, a guard with a weapon, a saint who smiles
  wrongly, a man who should be afraid and is not, the moment before something happens.
- ONE visible detail that turns the first impression around (a tear, a hand, a weapon, a smile, a shadow).
- Two honest readings of the same face: holy or to be feared, grief or hatred, protection or threat.
- Or a LEGEND about a named real person and the forbidden (a pact, a curse, a dream, a vision) that an old picture
  shows: "he claimed that ...".
What failed in this genre: famous but calm pictures (a pretty portrait, a quiet interior), plain history
(expeditions, battles, disasters), pictures where the horror is shown in full (blood, corpses, monsters).

### Hard limits
- The maker died before 1955 (public domain everywhere), or the work is older than 1900 by an unknown hand.
- It has its own article on the English Wikipedia; give the EXACT article title. Paintings, prints, drawings,
  illuminated manuscripts, sculptures. For a legend: the article of the person or of the legend, and it must have an
  old picture.
- Not mainly naked bodies, no gore.
- Not one of these (already made or already on the list): {AVOID}

### Where to look (spread the candidates over these, do not give twenty of one kind)
Symbolism and the fin de siecle (von Stuck, Khnopff, Redon, Schwabe, Malczewski, Vrubel) · academic painters of the
fallen and the damned (Cabanel, Bouguereau, Gerome, Delaroche) · Caravaggio and his followers, Ribera, Zurbaran, de
La Tour (one figure, one light) · Goya's black paintings and prints · Bosch and Bruegel details that work as a crop
· the Pre-Raphaelites and Waterhouse · Dore, Blake, Durer, Rembrandt prints · Russian and Polish history painting
(Repin, Kramskoi, Ge, Matejko) · medieval manuscripts and the devil · legends of musicians, alchemists, monks and
painters who "sold" something.
