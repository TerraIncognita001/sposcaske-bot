You judge ONE candidate for a dark art-history short video: an old picture (you are shown it) and the facts about
it. A calm voice will tell the viewer that something in this picture is not what it seems. Your job: say how well
this picture can hold a viewer who has never heard of it, on a phone, in the first three seconds and to the end.

The scale was built from 58 published videos of this genre. The 15 most watched took 75 % of all views. What set
them apart from the other 43 (share of the top 15 against share of the rest, and median views with / without):
- someone in the picture WATCHES (looks straight at the viewer, or is watched): 9 of 15 against 9 of 43; 1.29M / 0.29M
- one or two figures coming out of DARKNESS: 6 of 15 against 2 of 43; 1.65M / 0.39M
- the viewer can be spoken to as "you" inside the scene: 9 of 15 against 13 of 43; 0.89M / 0.29M
- a named person and a story he "claimed" or a legend with a source: 5 of 15 against 6 of 43; 0.93M / 0.35M
- violence SHOWN (blood, a killing, a corpse): 0 of 15 against 7 of 43; 0.26M / 0.60M
- a sacred or demonic figure by itself decided nothing (0.55M / 0.49M): what counts is what the figure DOES to you
- fame decided nothing: Girl with a Pearl Earring, Whistler's Mother and Christina's World are at the bottom

### Score six things (write one short reason for each, from what you SEE and from FACTS)
G  THE GAZE, 0-2.  2 = a figure looks straight out at the viewer, or the main figure is being watched by someone or
   something in the picture. 1 = eyes or a look matter but do not meet anyone. 0 = nobody looks.
D  DARKNESS AND ONE FIGURE, 0-2.  2 = one or two figures against a dark or empty ground, readable at phone size in
   one second. 1 = three to six figures, or a simple scene in daylight. 0 = a crowd, a landscape, small figures.
F  THE FLIP, 0-2.  2 = ONE thing, visible in a close crop, that contradicts the first impression (a tear on a
   perfect face, a spear in an angel's hand, a calm man among lions, a smile where there should be none). 1 = a
   detail exists but it has to be explained before it is seen. 0 = nothing to find.
T  THREAT WITHOUT VIOLENCE, 0-2.  2 = danger, the forbidden or the uncanny is present and NOTHING is shown
   happening (the moment before, the watcher, the one who should be afraid and is not). 1 = grief or aftermath.
   0 = nothing at stake, or the violence itself is shown (blood, a killing, a corpse, a monster in full view).
Y  YOU, 0-1.  1 = one honest question puts the viewer inside ("is he looking at you?", "would you have opened the
   door?", "which one do you see?"). 0 = the viewer stays outside.
S  A STORY WITH A NAME, 0-1.  1 = FACTS hold a story that pays off the flip and has a source that can be named
   (scripture, a legend about a named person, the artist's own words, a recorded event). 0 = only art history.
TOTAL = G + D + F + T + Y + S, from 0 to 10. A total of 0 is written as 1: the scale is 1 to 10.

### What the totals mean
9-10  make it first: the kind that took millions       7-8  make it       5-6  keep in reserve       1-4  never

### Disqualify (TOTAL = 0, say why) when
the facts are fewer than eight sentences; the picture is mostly naked bodies or shows gore (the platform would
restrict it); the picture is a photograph of living people; nothing in it can be recognised at phone size.

### The angle (only when TOTAL >= 5)
Give the ONE angle the video should take. It is the flip, not the most famous fact:
first_impression  what everyone sees in the first second (6-12 words)
detail            the one thing to point at, as a plain noun phrase ("the tear on his cheek"), and it must be
                  the STRONGEST one in the picture, usually in the face or the eyes, not a side detail
readings          the two ways to read it ("an angel mourning what he lost" / "the moment hatred is born")
question          the last question of the video, 6-16 words, beginning "So"
punch             three candidate punch words of 3-8 letters for the red word on screen
form              how this picture must be told (the form follows the picture):
                  "emotion"  the detail is in a FACE or in the eyes, and the video is about what that face feels
                             (a tear with anger under it, a gaze, a smile in the wrong place)
                  "scene"    the picture holds two parties, or a second figure or thing that most people do not
                             see at first and that changes what the scene means
                  "riddle"   the detail raises a question about what is happening (animals that do not attack, a
                             thing that is missing, an object that should not be there)
                  "legend"   the candidate is a story about a named person, not a painting to look into
